A Christmas recommendation

Four of Charlie, for the cottage

My four picks for framing for your parents. They're chosen to suit low beamed ceilings, warm limestone and smaller rooms rather than a white-walled gallery. Every size below is real and drawn to scale.

One of each: the cute one, the magnificent one, the fun one, and the place one.

50 cm
All four shown together to scale on a cottage wall with an inglenook mantel

Why these four, together

  • One hero, three companions. "Hopeful" is the only big one. The cottage rooms are small and the ceilings low, so one strong piece over the mantel and three modest ones elsewhere will look intentional, not crowded.
  • The same mount everywhere. All four use a warm off-white mount, not bright white. It sits kindly against limestone and lime-washed walls, and it ties the set together even when they hang in different rooms.
  • Woods that suit old houses. Oak, walnut and a painted sage feel at home with beams and stone, while black or chrome would look modern and cold there. "Lord of the Barn" gets a weathered grey oak to echo the Cotswold stone in the photo.
  • Four different Charlies. Cute, magnificent, fun and a sense of place, so the set tells a story rather than repeating one face.
  • A mix of shapes. Three landscape frames and one upright. The tall one breaks up the row and fits the narrow spaces cottages are full of, between windows, beams and doorways.
  • Standard sizes where possible. The three smaller ones fit off-the-shelf UK frames (40×50 and 30×40 cm, which is the size of the opening). The hero is best done by a framer, or in a ready-made 50×70 cm frame.

Ordering

  • Print from the full-resolution originals ("Download full resolution" in the gallery). Every photo on this page, crops included, prints at about 250 dpi or better at the size shown, so they'll be sharp.
  • Ask for a matte or lustre (semi-gloss) photo paper. Glossy prints pick up reflections from cottage windows and lamps.
  • Ask for anti-reflective or UV glass on the hero at least, as it's going over the fire and will catch the most light.
  • Mount apertures are usually cut about 5 mm smaller than the print so the edges are held. Any framer will do that automatically.
  • Some runners-up are shown cropped. If you swap one in, ask the printer to crop it the same way, or send Alex a screenshot.
